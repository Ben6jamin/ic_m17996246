For each possible key length from 3 through 19, I divided the ciphertext into columns and calculated the average IC. The likely fundamental key length is the candidate whose average column IC is close to the English IC, with a small penalty to avoid incorrectly selecting a multiple of the true key length. After selecting the length, I tested all 26 Caesar shifts for every column. Since IC is unchanged by a Caesar shift, I used chi-square comparison with standard English letter frequencies to identify each key letter. Smaller chi-square values indicate a closer match to English.

## Conclusion

The project showed that ordinary English has a nonuniform letter distribution and an IC noticeably above random text. A Vigenere cipher hides single-letter frequencies in the full ciphertext, but splitting the text at the correct key period creates Caesar-shifted English columns. Average IC reveals the period, while frequency scoring reveals the shifts and therefore the key.
